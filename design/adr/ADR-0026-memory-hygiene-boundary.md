# ADR-0026: The memory-hygiene boundary — Maknae-owned buffers of secret-class bytes zeroize; third-party codec and transport intermediates are a named residual

- **Status:** Accepted (maintainer-ruled 2026-09-22 — Option A, Cooky burn-down row 5; written 2026-09-25)
- **Date:** 2026-09-25
- **Deciders:** Alex Ackerman (maintainer)
- **Raised by:** [#342](https://github.com/darkhonor/maknae/issues/342), from `codex exec` round 2 on #241 (PR #341)

## Context

**Amended 2026-09-26 (#365):** this paragraph and decision 1.

Secret-class bytes, for this ADR — file content the subject reads and the model's write content (`SecretText` inside tool-call arguments, and the CLI's own `Bytes`, never on the kernel wire), prompt and reply text (`SecretText`), and the provider key — cross two codecs and one transport Maknae does not own, and they are held by three Maknae processes: `maknaed`, `maknae-egress`, and the subject's `maknae` / `maknae agent`. #241 established the rule for the buffers Maknae owns — zeroizing from allocation, never growing — and applied it to the kernel↔deputy frame buffers in both directions (the fixed-size encoders `encode_egress_frame_request` / `encode_egress_frame_reply` in `crates/maknae-proto/src/wire.rs` *(Renamed 2026-11-19 (#168, ADR-0038): `encode_egress_frame` / `encode_egress_socket_reply`; the frame is the tagged `EgressFrame`.)*, the kernel's zeroizing reply read) and to the provider request's Maknae-owned types (`SecretText` through `ChatMessage`, a zeroizing owner for the model's write bytes, the deputy's `text_of` in `crates/maknae-deputy/src/call.rs`, allocated once at the exact byte sum inside `Zeroizing`). It did not reach the provider reply inside `maknae-llm`, the client leg, or the provider key's path (decision 1). **Out of this ADR's scope, and not inventoried here:** the Vault authentication material the planes and `maknae enroll` hold. *(Amended 2026-10-02, #153 PR 4: except the wrapping token and the CLI's user-token copies, which decision 2 lists.)* The holding types keep the AppRole SecretID and the plane PKI leaf key in `Zeroizing` (`crates/maknae-vault/src/auth.rs`, `client.rs`), and the operator token is taken and dropped as `Zeroizing` (`operator.rs::new`, from `enroll::intake_token`); plain copies exist around them — among others, `VaultToken.client_token` (a plain `String` in `auth.rs`), `intake_token`'s `read_to_string` copy, the SecretIDs' YAML hand-off between `maknae enroll` and its helper (`ProvisionJob::to_yaml`, the helper's stdin `read_to_string`, `from_yaml`), the `--insecure-plaintext-secret` `to_vec`, every copy handed to vaultrs, its `X-Vault-Token` header and rustls's `CertifiedKey`. A separate decision inventories that material; this ADR does not. Its custody is [ADR-0018](ADR-0018-local-plane-authorization-deployment-model.md)'s and [ADR-0023](ADR-0023-runtime-loop-role-and-placement.md)'s, and its in-memory hygiene is a separate decision this ADR does not pre-empt. What lies inside dependencies for the bytes in scope, verified against the tree and ciborium 0.2.2's source on 2026-09-25:

1. **CBOR decode.** Every secret-class decode is `ciborium::from_reader` (`crates/maknae-proto/src/wire.rs`): `decode_request`, called from the kernel (`crates/maknae-kernel/src/run.rs`), which carries the prompt's `SecretText`; `decode_response`, called from the client (`bins/maknae/src/cli.rs`), which carries the prompt reply's `SecretText`; and the egress frame decoders. `SecretText`'s `Deserialize` is `String::deserialize`, which serde routes to `deserialize_string`; `Bytes` calls `deserialize_byte_buf` directly. In ciborium 0.2.2 (`src/de/mod.rs`) both build an **owned** buffer for every length — `String::new()` + `push_str`, `Vec::new()` + `extend_from_slice` — pulling chunks through a 4 KiB scratch. `from_reader` puts that scratch on its own stack (`[0; 4096]`) and does not wipe it; the public `from_reader_with_buffer` takes the scratch from the caller, so which scratch is used is Maknae's choice of entry point. So: the scratch keeps the most recent chunks decoded; a value over 4096 bytes, or an indefinite-length one delivered in segments, grows its buffer and may free each earlier copy unwiped; and a decode error drops the plain buffer before `Zeroizing` wraps it.
2. **Provider request.** `maknae_llm::chat_completion` (`crates/maknae-llm/src/client.rs`) sends the transcript with reqwest's `.json(req)`: `serde_json::to_vec` into a growing `Vec`, then a plainly owned HTTP body, then hyper's and rustls's write buffers (hyper may copy again depending on vectored-write support).
3. **Provider reply.** The reply arrives through rustls plaintext buffers, hyper's read buffers and reqwest's `chunk()` `Bytes`, and is un-escaped through serde_json's scratch buffer before it reaches Maknae's own types — in the deputy, and again in `maknae agent` when it parses tool arguments (`crates/maknae-agent/src/route.rs`).
4. **The provider key.** It is fetched with `vaultrs::kv2::read` (reqwest and serde_json again) into a plain `BTreeMap<String, String>` in `crates/maknae-vault/src/kv_io.rs`, copied out into `Zeroizing`, and the map dropped unwiped *(amended 2026-10-02, #153 PR 4: no longer — `kv_io.rs` is deleted and the key arrives by the unwrap leg, decision 2)*; then reqwest's `bearer_auth` builds the `Authorization` value with `format!("Bearer {token}")` — a plain `String`, then a `HeaderValue` (reqwest 0.13, `src/async_impl/request.rs`); `set_sensitive` does not zeroize the header storage. The key is in the deputy's memory in plain form on the Vault fetch and on the provider request.

Closing the third-party part of 1–4 means a bounded, non-growing CBOR string and byte-string decoder in `maknae-proto` (ciborium's growth and error path are inside ciborium; its scratch is not) and a request body type whose drop zeroizes (reqwest's `Body::from(Vec)` does not). That is a platform decision, not a slice of the loop, which is why #241 stopped at the crate boundary and asked for a ruling.

## Decision

### 1. The rule, and the Maknae-owned sites that do not meet it yet

Every buffer **Maknae owns** that holds secret-class bytes — in `maknaed`, `maknae-egress`, or the subject's `maknae` / `maknae agent` — **must** be zeroizing from allocation and must not grow. A new Maknae-owned buffer on a secret-class path that is a plain `String`/`Vec`, or that reallocates, is a defect from this ADR on. The sites that predate the rule and do not meet it, verified 2026-09-25:

**Amended 2026-09-26 (#365):** the `read_frame` and `encode_response_zeroizing` rows.

**Amended 2026-09-27 (#372):** the `read_frame` row is removed; `read_frame_zeroizing` is the only frame reader and hands its caller the body zeroizing.

**Amended 2026-10-02 (#153 PR 4):** the `kv_io.rs` row is removed with the file: the Egress Daemon no longer reads KV, and the provider key now arrives by the unwrap leg, whose Maknae-owned body and selected value are zeroizing (decision 2). The three new holders of secret-class bytes meet the rule and so have no row here: the Egress Daemon's seal private key (its PKCS#8 DER as `SealKeyDer` and the macOS hex form as `SealKeyHex` in `crates/maknae-vault/src/secret_source.rs`, each `Zeroizing` from allocation, pre-sized, never grown; redacted `Debug`, no `Display`), the opened wrapping token (`maknae_seal::open` returns it `Zeroizing`, and `WrappingToken::from_opened` holds it so), and the second copy of the user's Vault token that `maknae agent` makes for its plane client (`bins/maknae/src/agent.rs` `token_copy`: a `Zeroizing` buffer pre-sized to the token's length and never grown, held as a `UserToken`, whose `Debug` is redacted and which has no `Display`). Their third-party copies are decision 2's. The `cli.rs` row now names the field, because `Command::Agent` also carries `--provider`.

Each is converted by the change that next touches it, or filed as its own issue after the Cooky freeze at the maintainer's word. Until then they are **known**, not disputed.

Until this change those replies went through `encode_response` into a plain `Vec` that grew and was never wiped, a site no row listed; it is closed. The row's mis-sized-cap residual still applies to the prompt reply, which is sized by `reply_capacity`.

### 3. The threat-model sentence

**Amended 2026-09-26 (#365):** this paragraph and the Accepted bullet below.

**Amended 2026-09-28 (#76):** this paragraph and the process-isolation row.

A reader of a live `maknaed`, `maknae-egress` or subject-process memory — or of swap, or of a core dump where the host takes one (neither systemd unit sets `LimitCORE=0` and nothing sets `PR_SET_DUMPABLE`, so a Linux host's own core policy decides; neither launchd plist sets a core limit, so launchd's default, soft limit 0 on a stock system, decides) *(amended 2026-11-27, #752 PR 1: no longer holds for a process that holds a credential. Constructing any `MaknaeSecret` calls `deny_core_dumps`, which sets the soft `RLIMIT_CORE` to 0 and, on Linux, `PR_SET_DUMPABLE` 0, so `maknaed` and `maknae-egress` are not dumped by the host's policy once they hold a credential; the units still set no `LimitCORE=0`)* — obtains recent secret-class bytes from the buffers in decisions 1 and 2: prompt and reply text, write content and the provider key, which the provider sees anyway; and file content the subject reads — on the agent path the provider sees it too, as tool results; on the direct `maknae read` path nothing outside the host ever does. The Vault authentication material named out of scope above is exposed the same way. Maknae does not defend a process against a reader of its own memory. What bounds such a reader is process isolation — separate service uids and the credential custody [ADR-0023](ADR-0023-runtime-loop-role-and-placement.md) puts on them — and that bound holds today only on a packaged Linux install; on macOS the package ships both accounts (#287) and the deputy (#394), and the bound is pending the macOS acceptance run (#76) *(2026-09-28, #76: on a notarized install `maknaed` ran as `_maknae`, and the deputy ran as its own launchd job, its uid not recorded; the memory-reader bound itself was not measured, and no issue tracks measuring it)*; the custody assertion is not yet built (#242). Zeroization narrows what a later reader finds, and it is applied wherever decision 1 is met.

### 4. Not decided here

Option B (own the codec and the body) and Option C (B for the kernel↔deputy CBOR leg only, since the provider leg's bytes leave the trust boundary by design) stay candidates; the #241 review's recommendation to consider C first stands. `LimitCORE=0` on both units (and its launchd equivalent) is the obvious cheap narrowing of decision 3 and is likewise unfiled. Nothing is filed while the Cooky milestone is frozen; whoever takes any of it up cites this ADR and carries a T1 change's mutation-gate obligations.

## Consequences

- **Positive:** one home for the claim. A reviewer who finds an unwiped intermediate checks decision 2 before filing, and an unwiped Maknae-owned buffer checks decision 1's table — a listed site is known, an unlisted one is a defect or a missing row, and either way it is recorded here.
- **Accepted:** the residual is real, and it is larger than "the codec": a memory read, a core dump or swap yields recent prompt text, file content the subject reads, write content and, in the deputy, the provider key — and, outside this ADR's scope, the Vault authentication material the planes and `maknae enroll` hold. This was already true for prompt text since the prompt leg shipped (#172, #240, #264); the ADR names it rather than narrows it.
- **Obligation on later work:** a change that touches a decision-1 site converts it and removes the row; a change that adds or removes a third-party path on a secret-class route updates decision 2 in the same PR.

## Amendment 2026-11-27 — delivered credentials and core dumps (#751)

A credential a front end, or later the launch helper (#755), holds to deliver to a tool is secret-class. It is held only as `maknae_io::credential::DeliveredCredential`, and it travels to the helper only inside `LaunchCredentials`. Both meet decision 1: the value lives in a `Zeroizing` buffer, the encoded frame is allocated once at its exact length and zeroized, and the receive buffer is zeroized from allocation. `DeliveredCredential` has no `Display`, `Clone`, `PartialEq` or serde, and its `Debug` prints a fixed marker; compile-fail doctests pin each. `LaunchCredentials` has no `Display` (pinned by a compile-fail doctest), cannot derive the others while it holds `DeliveredCredential`s, and its `Debug` prints the slots, never a value. `deliver_to` refuses an empty value and one over 64 KiB, so every bundle it builds is one the receiver accepts *(amended 2026-11-27, #752 PR 1: `deliver_to` no longer checks the value. The refusal belongs to `MaknaeSecret`'s constructor, which every `DeliveredCredential` is built from, so a bundle still holds only values the receiver accepts; `DeliverErrorKind::{EmptyValue, ValueTooLong}` are gone)*, `send` and `receive` bound the whole frame by the stream's timeout, waiting with `poll` and never changing the stream's options.

Decision 3's core-dump exposure narrows for those processes, and only for them:

- `maknae_io::deny_core_dumps` lowers the soft `RLIMIT_CORE` to 0 and, on Linux, sets the process non-dumpable (`PR_SET_DUMPABLE` 0). It reapplies on every call, since a uid or gid change resets dumpability, and a failure is sticky. Constructing a `DeliveredCredential` calls it *(amended 2026-11-27, #752 PR 1: no longer; `into_delivered` is infallible and does not call it again, because the `MaknaeSecret` it consumes called it when it was built)*, `receive` calls it before reading a byte, and a holder calls it before fetching a credential. It moves only the soft limit, so it needs no `setrlimit` permission under a confined domain.
- The agent daemon's unit (`maknae-agent@.service`) sets `LimitCORE=0`, which `packaging/isolation-contract.md` pins.
- `maknaed`'s and `maknae-egress`'s units are unchanged. Decision 3's sentence about them still holds *(amended 2026-11-27, #752 PR 1: no longer; both now deny core dumps from their first `MaknaeSecret` on, though their units are still unchanged)*, and decision 4's `LimitCORE=0` on both units stays unfiled.

No front end holds a tool credential yet (the tool path is #479 and #168), so no production process calls `deny_core_dumps` today. The narrowing takes effect when the first holder is built. *(Amended 2026-11-27, #752 PR 1: no longer holds. Every `MaknaeSecret` constructor calls `deny_core_dumps`, so `maknaed`, `maknae-egress`, the `maknae` CLI and `maknae enroll` call it when they build their first credential.)*

Residuals of this amendment (decision 2 lists third-party intermediates; these are process-level):

- A piped core handler (`core_pattern` beginning with `|`) that does not honour the soft limit (`%c`) stores the dump of a process that is still dumpable: on macOS, or on Linux when a uid or gid change has reset dumpability since the last `deny_core_dumps` call. A non-dumpable process is not dumped.
- The window between fetching a value and constructing the `DeliveredCredential`, when the value sits in the fetching client's own buffers. A holder shortens it by calling `deny_core_dumps` before the fetch.
- Non-dumpable is Linux-only. On macOS only the soft core limit applies; nothing equivalent to `PR_SET_DUMPABLE` is set.

## Amendment 2026-11-27 — one secret type (#752 PR 1)

Every credential Maknae holds is now a `maknae_io::MaknaeSecret<B>` (`crates/maknae-io/src/secret.rs`), except a tool credential being delivered, which stays a `DeliveredCredential` (the #751 amendment above). The type's storage is a sealed parameter: `String` for text, `Vec<u8>` (`MaknaeSecretBytes`) for bytes, `[u8; N]` for a fixed-length key. Its rules are written once, in one `impl`, instead of once per type:

- the value lives in a `Zeroizing` buffer the type owns, and no method grows or mutates it; `expose()` returns a borrow;
- there is no `Display`, `Clone`, `PartialEq`, `Serialize`, `Deref` or `AsRef`, and `Debug` prints `MaknaeSecret(<redacted>)`; compile-fail doctests pin `Display`, `Clone`, `PartialEq`, `Serialize` and the private field;
- construction refuses an empty value and one over 65,536 bytes (`MAX_SECRET_BYTES`), and `vault_token` adds the Vault token rule (1..=1024 bytes of `[A-Za-z0-9._-]`); a tighter limit a site applied before stays at that site;
- construction calls `deny_core_dumps` first, and a failure there is returned as `SecretError::CoreDumps`, which each crate's mapping keeps as its own core-dump error rather than folding it into a validation error;
- ownership of a fixed-length key leaves the type only through `MaknaeSecret<[u8; N]>::into_zeroizing`, which consumes the secret and returns its `Zeroizing` array. Two residuals remain, as they did for `GraphKey`: `[u8; N]` is `Copy`, so `*s.expose()` makes a plain, unwiped copy, and a move of a `MaknaeSecret<[u8; N]>` is a bitwise copy that can leave the key's bytes in the stack slot it left.

The seven per-feature types the 2026-11-27 survey found (`UserToken`, `KvSecret`, `WrappingToken`, `ServiceToken`, `AgentSecret`, `Password`, `SealKeyHex`), with `SealKeyDer` and `GraphKey`, are deleted, and so are the two copies of the JSON secret visitor (`api_shape::SecretStr`, the one in `maknae-deputy/src/mint/grant.rs`); `de_zeroizing_string` in `maknae-io` replaces both. The credentials the survey found with no wrapper moved to the type too: the LLM provider key in `maknae-deputy`, the minted GitHub tokens and the App JWT, `Held.token` in `maknae-deputy/src/reach/drive.rs`, `AppRoleAuth.secret_id`, the enrollment SecretID, and `VaultToken.client_token`, which was a plain `String` and is now a `MaknaeSecret`.

Effect on the sites this ADR names:

- Decision 1, 2026-10-02 amendment: the Egress Daemon's seal key is a `MaknaeSecretBytes` (the PKCS#8 DER, formerly `SealKeyDer`) and a `MaknaeSecret` (the macOS hex form, formerly `SealKeyHex`); the pre-sized hex encode and decode are crate-private helpers in `secret_source.rs` whose capacity tests check that they never grow. The opened wrapping token is a `MaknaeSecret` built by `MaknaeSecret::from_opened` (formerly `WrappingToken::from_opened`). `maknae agent`'s `token_copy` (`bins/maknae/src/agent.rs`) still writes into a `Zeroizing` buffer pre-sized to the token's length, and is held as a `MaknaeSecret`, not a `UserToken`. Each still meets decision 1.
- Decision 2, the AWS-LC row: `SealPrivateKey::to_pkcs8_der` returns a `MaknaeSecretBytes`, so `maknae enroll` holds the generated key's DER as one across the custody write. Before this change it was a bare `Zeroizing<Vec<u8>>`. AWS-LC's own buffers are unchanged.
- Decision 2, the unwrap-leg row: `select_kv_field` returns a `MaknaeSecret` and refuses an empty value, which the decoder accepted before. The transport and serde_json residuals in that row are unchanged.
- Context, the plain copies of Vault authentication material: `VaultToken.client_token` is a `MaknaeSecret`; `intake_token`'s `--token-file` read fills a buffer that is zeroizing from allocation, sized once at `MAX_SECRET_BYTES + 1` and never grown (`read_token_bytes`, refusing one longer than the cap), so a pipe's read frees no unwiped prefix, and the trimmed token is one exact-length copy into a `Zeroizing<String>`; the plaintext-path SecretID reader (`client.rs::read_secret_credential`), which returned a plain `String`, returns `Zeroizing<String>`, moved into the `MaknaeSecret` with no copy. The `--insecure-plaintext-secret` `to_vec` (`bins/maknae/src/enroll/mod.rs`), the copies handed to vaultrs, the `X-Vault-Token` header and rustls's `CertifiedKey` remain.

The password becomes readable outside `maknae-vault`. `Password::expose` was `pub(crate)`; `MaknaeSecret::expose` is `pub`, and `bins/maknae/src/tty.rs` builds the password. The one-type design accepts this: a crate-private reader would need a second type, which is what this change removes. Any crate holding the value can read it, as for every other credential the type holds.

A value exists outside the type only between its read or decode and its constructor: a `Zeroizing<String>` or `Zeroizing<Vec<u8>>` that a reader returns (the keychain reads, `read_sealed_trimmed`, `read_secret_credential`, `pick_kv_field`, `open_forge_seal`) or that a serde DTO field holds (`LoginAuthDto.client_token`, `WrapInfoDto.token`, through `de_zeroizing_string`). In `maknaed`, `into_zeroizing` hands its array to `maknae_state::envelope::WrappingKey::new`, which builds the opaque AEAD key from it. Each Maknae-side buffer keeps it zeroizing, and the array is dropped zeroizing after the call. What the `security` and `systemd-creds` subprocesses and their pipes hold is decision 2's, as for the seal key. The typed-input buffers (`PasswordLine`, `TokenLine`) are zeroizing, pre-sized and never grown, and `finish` builds the `MaknaeSecret`. These meet decision 1 and need no row.

## Amendment 2026-11-28 — the value filter (#752 PR 2)

A Maknae process that holds a credential can now find that credential's value in bytes it is about to release, through one type: `maknae_io::ValueFilter` (`crates/maknae-io/src/filter.rs`). It matches known values only. It never matches by name, prefix or shape, so a value it was not given is never found.

**Building it.** `ValueFilter::builder()` borrows the values (`secret(label, &MaknaeSecret)`, or a credential slot through `LaunchCredentials::value_filter`), and `build()` copies them once into the filter's own buffers. For each value it keeps a set of *forms*: the raw bytes; for a value of 16 to 4,096 bytes, also hex in lower and upper case, percent-encoding in upper and lower case (every byte outside `[A-Za-z0-9._~-]` as `%XX`), and base64 in the standard and URL-safe alphabets at each of the three byte alignments. A base64 form is encoded without padding and then trimmed: the leading `ceil(4a/3)` characters for alignment `a` and, when `(a + n) % 3 != 0`, the last character, because those characters carry bits from outside the value. A form identical to one already kept is dropped, so a percent form equal to the raw form is not stored twice. The filter then builds a *position index*: for each window length (16, 24 and 32) a table of `(form, offset)` pairs, one per distinct window of bytes per source, sorted by the window's bytes and then by source. The index holds positions only; no hash of a value is stored. A scan binary-searches each window of the input in each table, compares a short form directly against the input at every offset, and marks the bytes a hit covers. It never extends a match.

**Hygiene.** Every buffer that holds value bytes or is derived from them is `Zeroizing` and sized once at its final length: each form, the zero-prefixed input and full output of each base64 encoding, the three index tables (which keep their pre-deduplication capacity, since shrinking would free an unwiped allocation), and the prefix-function table of the tail rule, which is allocated per `mask_in_place` call and dropped at its end. The index tables are sorted with `sort_unstable_by`, which sorts in place with no heap scratch; that choice is held by review, since a stable sort's scratch is observable only through a counting global allocator, which would need `unsafe` (ADR-0027). Nothing grows after `build()`. The coverage map and the list of matched sources hold no value bytes and are plain. The text `redact_text` returns is plain too: it holds no indexed window, though a fragment of a value shorter than a window stays in it. There is no public exit: no method returns a value, a form, or anything derived from their bytes; `Debug` prints `ValueFilter(<N> values)`, and `Screened`'s `Debug` prints only its variant and a length. The filter has no `Clone`, `Display`, `Serialize` or `PartialEq`.

**What it offers.**

- `screen(output, hook)` takes tool output by value and returns `Screened::Clean(output)` or `Screened::Withheld { len }`. On a match it calls `LeakHook::leaked` once, listing each matched source once (two values under one label are one source), and withholds the whole output.
- `mask_in_place(buf)` overwrites each covered byte with `*`, so the buffer's length does not change and a `Zeroizing<Vec<u8>>` never grows, and returns the matched sources. It alone applies the tail rule below.
- `matches(bytes)` reports whether any value is present.
- `redact_text(s)` returns a new string in which each covered run, widened to whole characters, is replaced by `<redacted>`.

**The three amendments to #752's specification.**

1. *Windows are sized in plaintext.* A window covers at least 16 bytes of the value: 16 bytes of the raw form, 32 characters of hex, 24 characters of base64 (18 bytes). For raw, hex and base64, a public vendor prefix shorter than 16 bytes (`sk-ant-api03-`, `github_pat_11`, a JWT header) is never a match on its own. A percent window is 16 characters, which can cover as few as 6 plaintext bytes, because an encoded byte takes 3 characters. The public-prefix guarantee therefore does not extend to percent forms; a percent false match only withholds or masks too much. A form shorter than its window is matched whole: a raw value of 8 to 15 bytes, and an encoded form of a short value (base64 of a 16- to 18-byte value at some alignments, percent forms of short values). The direct comparison is bounded by the needle's length, which is under 32 characters.
2. *Byte buffers are masked with `*`; text gets markers.* A byte buffer is masked in place so it keeps its length and never reallocates; `redact_text` returns a new string with `<redacted>` markers.
3. *One hook call per matching `screen`.* The issue's "fires once per matched slot" is met by one report per slot, all in one call: `LeakHook::leaked` receives each matched source once.

**Thresholds and limits.** Each is a named limit, not a defect to re-file.

- A value shorter than 8 bytes (`MIN_VALUE`) is not matched at all. The builder counts it in `skipped()`.
- A value of 8 to 15 bytes (`WHOLE_BELOW` is 16) gets its raw form only and is matched whole.
- Encoded forms exist only for values of 16 to 4,096 bytes (`MAX_ENCODED_VALUE`); a longer value is matched raw only.
- `LaunchCredentials::value_filter` indexes `CredentialSlot::EnvVar` slots only. `GitCredentialHelper`, `CargoCredentialProvider`, `Netrc` and `File` slots can hold structured lines or whole files whose public boilerplate would withhold innocent output, so they are skipped and counted in `skipped()`. #479 decides how they are matched.
- When the value sits inside a larger base64 blob, the character that straddles its leading edge is unmasked: one character, carrying 4 of the value's bits at alignment 1 and 2 at alignment 2 (the other trimmed leading characters encode only the bytes before the value). So is the character that straddles its trailing edge, when `(a + n) % 3 != 0`.
- At a truncation, an encoded form cut to fewer characters than its window is not masked; the tail rule covers raw forms only.
- **The tail rule** (`mask_in_place` only): for each raw form, the longest prefix of the value that the buffer ends with is masked, found with a prefix function over the form in time linear in the value's length. The masked tail's length tells a reader how many of the value's leading bytes the buffer ended with. That is the same prefix oracle the deleted `maknae_llm::redact` had.
- `screen` has no tail rule. Tool output cut at a truncation can end in up to 15 leading bytes of a value and still be `Clean`.
- A lax percent encoder, one that encodes a different set of bytes than the strict form, is caught only where its output agrees with a stored form over a whole window, that is, through fragments.
- Public prefixes are not matches (amendment 1), except as the percent case allows.
- Encodings outside the known forms are not matched: line-wrapped base64 (MIME, `openssl base64`), separated hex (`xxd`, `hexdump -C`), and JSON string escaping (`\/`, `\uXXXX`).
- Known values only: anything the filter was not given passes.
- A rewrite can rebuild a value across its own boundary (a value containing `<redacted>` or `*`). After rewriting, `redact_text` scans its output again and, if it still matches, returns `<redacted>` alone, or an empty string when `<redacted>` itself matches; `mask_in_place` scans the buffer again and applies the tail rule again and, if either still finds the value, overwrites all of it with `*`. A value whose raw form contains 16 consecutive `*`, or an 8–15-byte value made only of `*`, still matches an all-`*` buffer; the output is then entirely `*`.

**Fail closed.** `build()` returns `Err(FilterError::TooManyValues)` past `MAX_VALUES` (1,024) values and `Err(FilterError::ValueTooLong)` for a value longer than `MAX_SECRET_BYTES`; a filter that dropped a known value without saying so would be fail-open. `LaunchCredentials` holds at most 16 entries, each a `MaknaeSecret`, so neither error is reachable there. The masking call sites treat a filter that cannot cover the value as an error too: `mask_body` (`crates/maknae-deputy/src/call.rs`) masks the provider's error body only when the filter built, is not empty and skipped nothing, and otherwise overwrites the whole body with `*`; `redact_error` (`bins/maknae/src/enroll/agent.rs`), through `covering`, replaces the whole error with the `WITHHELD` message under the same three conditions, and otherwise runs `redact_text` over each of the error's text fields, keeping its variant, then replaces the error with its redacted `Display` text if the rendered error still matches, and with the `WITHHELD` message if that replacement's `Display` or `Debug` still matches. `screen` itself does not fail closed when the filter skipped a slot: a skipped git-credential slot would otherwise withhold every output. Its caller decides, as the two masking sites do by blanking on a skip; for tool output that caller is #479's.

**Cost.** Per input byte, the scan does one binary search per window length, each step comparing at most that window's length in bytes, so about (16 + 24 + 32) × ⌈log2 m⌉ byte comparisons, where `m` is the index's size. It then walks the run of equal entries, at most one per source since the index keeps one entry per window per source, and compares each whole form directly, at most its needle length (under 32 bytes) each. The tail rule adds work linear in the value's length per raw form. The tests measure the work on three adversarial 1 MiB inputs (a value repeated, a run of one byte against a 4,096-byte run value, and the first 14 bytes of a 15-byte value repeated) and assert it stays under `WORK_C` × n × ⌈log2 m⌉ with `WORK_C` = 16; on 2026-11-28 they measured about 10.1, 11.0 and 2.0 times n × ⌈log2 m⌉. These figures are for a filter of one value. The whole-form comparisons and the coverage fill scale with the number of whole forms, which production bounds: `LaunchCredentials` holds at most 16 entries, and each masking site builds a filter of one value. The post-check scans are not in the work counter; a clean call pays one extra scan of the same length (the bound at most doubles). Every loop terminates by construction: each is a loop over a range, or is bounded by its pattern's length.

**Sites.** `maknae_llm::redact` (`crates/maknae-llm/src/wire.rs`) and enroll's `redact` and `scrub_secret` (`bins/maknae/src/enroll/agent.rs`) are deleted. The provider wire's error body is masked by `mask_body`; enroll's sealing errors, in `agent.rs` and `egress_minting.rs`, are redacted by `redact_error` with `secret_filter`. Nothing in production calls `screen` yet: #479 wires it to tool output, and #168 PR C supplies the revocation behind `LeakHook`.
